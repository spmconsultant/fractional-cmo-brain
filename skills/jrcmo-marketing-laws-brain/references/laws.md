# The JRcmo Marketing Laws

Every law is named **JRcmo Law #N — Name**, from JRcmo Law #1 to JRcmo Law #20.

A LAW is how marketing works whether you like it or not — a smart competitor would be forced to agree. Every law is taught the same way: **WHAT** is true, **WHY** it's true, and **HOW** to use it. Then you go do it.

These laws come from 25+ years of real campaigns: $1.5B+ in ad placements since 1999, 100,000+ campaigns and 300+ marketing plans.

---

## JRcmo Law #1 — Two Meanings of ROI
- **What:** "Return on Investment" is a backward-looking ratio. "Result of Investment" asks the forward-looking question: what changed because you invested that would not have happened otherwise?
- **Why:** Attribution tells you who got the credit. Incrementality tells you what the money actually caused.
- **How:** Track conversion rate, sales velocity, retention, price realization and CAC/LTV. A campaign with zero leads can still produce a result — it can prove the website is the failure point.

## JRcmo Law #2 — The Four Core Marketing Abilities
| Ability | What the buyer needs | Barrier |
|---|---|---|
| DEPEND | Trust | Risk |
| LIKE | An emotional pull toward you | Emotional resistance |
| RELATE | "This is for someone like me" | Relevance |
| KNOWLEDGE | Understanding | Confusion |
- **What:** A purchase happens only when all four are present.
- **Why:** Over-investing in one can't make up for missing another.
- **How:** Use it as a diagnostic grid. When sales stall, find the missing ability and fix that barrier first.

## JRcmo Law #3 — Leverage Before Scale
- **What:** Small, controlled changes to a proven driver produce outsized gains without adding cost, risk or complexity.
- **Leverage hierarchy (highest → lowest):** Message > Audience > Offer > Experience > Spend. Spend is the LOWEST lever — it amplifies whatever is already broken upstream.
- **How:** Change ONE variable at a time, measure the lift against a baseline, lock the gain, then move to the next.

## JRcmo Law #4 — The Law of Platitudes
- **What:** What everyone agrees with persuades no one.
- **Acid Test:** Could a direct competitor say this without changing a word? If yes, delete it.
- **How:** Replace platitudes with copy that is Specific, Risk-bearing, Verifiable, Segmented and Opinionated.
- **Dead words (never use):** Lowest Prices, Best Service, Highest Quality, Most Professional, Largest Selection, Biggest in State, Family Owned, Most Reliable, In Business Since 19XX.

## JRcmo Law #5 — The Law of Thirds
- **What:** Roughly a third converts at each stage — impressions to leads, leads to qualified, qualified to closed.
- **Realistic benchmarks:** Impression → Click 0.5–3% · Click → Lead 10–30% · Lead → Close 20–35%.
- **How:** Work BACKWARD from the closed deals you need. Improving impression→click from 2% to 3% cuts the impressions you need by 33%. Improving lead→close from 30% to 40% cuts the leads you need by 25%.

## JRcmo Law #6 — The Law of Diminishing Returns
- **What:** Each additional dollar produces less than the one before it.
- **Warning signs:** CPA rising faster than volume · frequency above 3–5 · revenue flat while spend climbs.
- **How:** Judge by INCREMENTAL cost (the last dollar), not BLENDED cost (the average, which hides saturation). Scale until incremental CPA reaches your allowable CPA.
- **The one exception — the 48-Hour Haunt:** high-frequency retargeting for 24–48 hours after a site visit is allowed to break the frequency cap.

## JRcmo Law #7 — The Girlfriend Effect (Relevance Before Identity)
- **What:** Nobody cares who you are until they care what you can do for them. Don't lead with your name, logo, founding year or credentials.
- **First-Sentence Test:** Cover the opening line. If it names the company, the founder, years in business or the service category before naming something the reader wants, it fails.
- **How:** Open with the reader's outcome or tension. The ad sells the solution; your name shows up at the end as the domain. They learn who you are after they trust you.

## JRcmo Law #8 — The Check-Writing Test
- **What:** Marketing is tied to revenue or tied to nothing.
- **The test:** When the owner signs the monthly check, what number does their mind land on? If it lands on a number they care about, they sign without friction. If it lands on nothing, the account is lost no matter how good the work was.
- **How:** Build every plan backward from revenue to leads. Agree on one KPI the owner will remember at signing time, get it in writing, and make it revenue. "5 million impressions" is never a finish line.
- **KPI Lock:** open every meeting with "this is the main KPI — has it changed?", close by confirming it, and report against it next time. If the KPI moves, the work moves with it.

## JRcmo Law #9 — Pricing Obscure
- **What:** An odd price ($277, not $300) signals you worked out the real cost and pushed it down.
- **Why:** A lower entry price creates commitment and consistency — people who start small tend to continue.

## JRcmo Law #10 — Outcome Without Consequence
- **What:** A headline that states a neutral outcome tells the reader nothing. "Stop backups now" fails — it doesn't say what happens if you don't.
- **How:** Name the DAMAGE the reader is about to suffer. "We can fix your pipes without tearing out your floors."
- **Test:** Does it create a tear, a scare, or a pain point — or is it just a description of the service?
- **Companion — pain and consequence beat speed and features:** buyers aren't afraid of slow; they're afraid of damage.
- **Companion — headline-to-visual congruence:** if the headline says "don't dig up your floors," the image can't be a pipe.

## JRcmo Law #11 — The Default Wins
- **What:** Showing up consistently in the right place beats being clever occasionally. Be present often enough and you catch the demand that's already there.
- **How:** Make the action you want the easy default for the buyer. Commit to consistency (posting, SEO, showing up) — compounding presence drives the result, not any single clever move.

## JRcmo Law #12 — Stale Proof Is False Proof
- **What:** Proof only counts if it's specific and current. An undated testimonial or an old stat ("doubled in 2019") reads as false — if it were still true, you'd have a current number.
- **How:** Pin every claim to a specific, current metric and a date. If you can't show a fresh number, don't lean on a stale one.

## JRcmo Law #13 — Fresh Data or No Data
- **What:** If you don't keep updating the strategy, you never generate new data to make decisions from. Stale data leads to stale decisions.
- **How:** Keep the strategy live. Change one thing, read the result, decide, repeat.

## JRcmo Law #14 — Clarity of Conversions
- **What:** Something set as a "conversion" isn't necessarily a lead. Engagement goals (time on page, pages per visit) are fine to track, but they are not leads.
- **How:** Before reading any conversion number, find out what it's actually measuring. A real lead is always a whole number.

## JRcmo Law #15 — Don't Trust the Zero
- **What:** Zero reported conversions doesn't mean nothing happened.
- **Example:** Joshua's own Google Ads showed zero attributed conversions for years — yet shutting them off took leads to zero. The ads educated buyers who came back later through other channels.
- **How:** Before you kill a channel showing zero, test what happens to total leads when it's off.

## JRcmo Law #16 — Don't Trust the Fraction
- **What:** A conversion count with a decimal (0.44, 0.62) means the platform is counting something that isn't a real conversion. You can't have a fraction of a person.
- **How:** When you see a decimal, find what's being tracked (page views, engaged sessions, time on page) and demote it to secondary or remove it.

## JRcmo Law #17 — Own the Keys
- **What:** The business must OWN one central account that everything connects to — domain, hosting, analytics, ad accounts, tracking, every login.
- **Why:** So no agency, consultant or employee can ever hold the business hostage.
- **How:** One dedicated Google account owns everything. The owner holds the password from day one. Nothing connects around it.

## JRcmo Law #18 — Speed to Lead
- **What:** Speed wins. The business that responds first usually wins the customer.
- **Caveat:** Speed only helps once the data is right — fast follow-up on the wrong leads just loses money faster.

## JRcmo Law #19 — AI Cites What It Can Quote and Verify
- **What:** AI assistants (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google's AI answers) don't rank pages; they assemble answers from passages they can lift cleanly and confirm elsewhere. If your answer can't be quoted in a sentence or two — or nobody else says the same thing about you — you aren't in the answer.
- **Test:** Cover everything but the first 150 words of the page. Could an AI answer the searcher's question from just that? If not, it fails.
- **How:** Answer the question plainly up top, keep your business facts identical everywhere online, and date your claims.

## JRcmo Law #20 — The Confidence Gap
- **What:** The business stands on one cliff, the buyer on the other, with a gap in between. Marketing's job is to build the bridge they walk across.
- **Why:** The bridge is made of trust — but not the word "trust." Platitudes fall into the gap. Specific, checkable details hold weight.
- **The Plank Test:** For every claim, ask "could the buyer check this before they cross?" If yes, it's a plank. If no, it's decoration and it breaks under weight.
- **How:** The gap is the four barriers from JRcmo Law #2 (risk, confusion, relevance, emotional resistance). Each needs its own planks. The bridge hangs from four cables — Depend, Like, Relate, Knowledge. Cut one and it drops.

## The foundation: SAY IT. PLACE IT. LAND IT.
All marketing comes down to two things:
1. **Strategic Message (Say It)** — not what you say, but what the buyer *perceives*. Figure out what they're already thinking, say it back to them, and they'll agree and engage.
2. **Tactical Placement (Place It)** — how you distribute the message. Placement bends the message: every channel has its own rules (radio needs the phone number and website said 3–4 times in 30 seconds; a search ad has one line to land).
3. **Land It** — message and placement only matter if they land with the right buyer.

## The Law of the Knockdown List
**"Marketing money must be spent in inverse proportion to uncertainty."** Disqualify prospects cheaply first; spend the expensive money only on the ones who are left.
