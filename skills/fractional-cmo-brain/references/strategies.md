# Strategies

Strategies are plays proven across many businesses, ready to run on yours.

**The test for a strategy:** it works for a pest control company and a law firm alike. It has been proven across industries including pest control, law firms, clinics, contractors, and IT and software companies.

---

## See It. Test It. Read It. Scale It.
**Your budget doesn't get a guess. It gets a test.**

1. **See it:** find the constraint using the Diagnostic Order principle.
2. **Test it:** change one variable against a clear baseline.
3. **Read it:** measure the incremental lift, using real leads (whole numbers) and the trend, not blended numbers.
4. **Scale it:** add spend only after leverage is found, and stop when incremental CPA reaches allowable CPA (Laws 3 and 6).

## The Knockdown List
**Spend pennies to disqualify, then spend real money only on the buyers left standing.**

Use low-cost touches to filter out buyers who won't fit before committing serious budget, then put the real spend behind the qualified few. This applies Law 5: work backward from the deals you need, and cut waste early in the funnel where it's cheapest.

## Answer First, Then Lead Them In
**Win the question your buyer actually asks, then walk them to the service you want to sell.**

Build pages that answer the buyer's real question plainly at the top, so both people and AI can quote it (Law 19). Then guide the reader from that answer to the related service. Lead with relevance, then identity (Law 7).

## The Small First Yes
Against an incumbent, ask for one order, one job or one sample, make the result measurable, then climb from trial to proof to a standing relationship (Laws 9 and 11).

## The Quarterly Proof Sweep
Once a quarter, check every public number on the website, Google profile and social bios on the same day. Replace expiring claims with fixed anchors ("since 2008"). See Law 12.

---

When the user's situation doesn't match one of these strategies, build the recommendation from the laws and principles directly and say so. Don't present an invented play as a proven JRCMO strategy.
