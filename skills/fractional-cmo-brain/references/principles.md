# Principles

Principles are the stances the JRCMO framework takes that another CMO might not.

**The test for a principle:** a competitor could reasonably choose differently. If a smart competitor is forced to agree, it's a law instead.

Principles decide what gets asked first, what gets tested, and what gets cut.

---

## Spend is the lowest-leverage lever
Don't touch the budget first. Diagnose before a dollar moves. More spend amplifies whatever is broken upstream (see Laws 3 and 6).

## Diagnostic Order
**Traffic with no leads has four possible causes. Check them out of order and you fix the wrong one.**

Run the checks in this order:
1. **Impression share:** are you being seen?
2. **Time on site by channel:** is the traffic real?
3. **Pages per visit:** are visitors engaging?
4. **Only then, the call to action and the offer.**

If the first three are healthy and leads are still zero, the problem is the page and the offer, not the media. Fix one thing, measure it, and lock the gain.

## The Other Nine
**Everyone argues about the lead that closed. Your money is in the nine that didn't.**

Review the leads that didn't close and ask why. The answer often points to the website, the offer or the follow-up, not the ads.

## One Story Everywhere
**An AI that finds three versions of your business trusts none of them.**

Your website, Google Business Profile, social bios and directory listings should state the same facts the same way: services, service area, years, proof. Inconsistency costs both buyer trust and AI citations (see Laws 12 and 19).

## Data Before Speed
Fix where leads land, how their source is tracked, and what counts as qualified, before racing to respond faster (see Law 18).

## Change one variable at a time
Change one thing, measure it against the baseline, and lock the gain before changing the next. Two changes at once means you'll never know which one worked (see Law 3).

## Verify before you decide
Count leads from the CRM, read the trend instead of the absolute number, and know what every conversion measures before you judge a campaign (see Laws 14, 15 and 16).
